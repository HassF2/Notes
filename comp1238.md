## Week 1 - History
- Mesopotamia is where the first known writing appeared.
- The Babylonians used base 60 instead of base 10.
- Gilgamesh searched for immortality after his friend died.

## Week 2 - Bits and Bytes
- Computers store everything as bits
- Morse code can be used short and long signals to communicate information
- SSDs, HDDs, and SD cards are non-volatile, meaning they keep data without power.

## Week 3 - Text Structure
- Ancient books used different font sizes and layout to seperate the main text from commentary
- Newspapers use different text sizes, headings, and images to make information easy to scan.
- Lines are numbered in editors so developers can quick find errors
- Alphanumeric characters are letters and numbers.
- Non-alphanumeric characters include symbols like @, #, $, and &
  
## Week 4 - Keyboards and Editors
- Your keyboard, mouse, desk, and chair are your tools of the trade.
- A comfortable workspace helps prevent pain and strain during long computer sessions.
- Repetitive Strain Injury (RSI) can develop from repetitive movements and poor ergonomics.
- The IBM Model M keyboard (1985) helped establish the modern keyboard layout.
- Touch typing improves speed, accuracy, and concentration.
  
## Week 5 - CLI
- The CLI (Command-Line Interface) lets you interact with a computer by typing commands.
- The terminal is basically a text-based conversation with your computer.
- Early computers used keyboards and printers to send commands and receive responses.
- Commands were often shortened to save time, which is why some command names seem cryptic today.
- A prompt shows you where you are and lets you know the computer is ready for a command.
- The Bandit game teaches command-line skills through security challenges.
